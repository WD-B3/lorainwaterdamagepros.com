Water Damage in a Rental in Lorain, OH: Tenant and Landlord Steps

When water hits a rental, both sides have things to do. This guide covers notice, repairs, belongings and records, in plain language.

By the Lorain Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

Water damage in a rental raises the same questions as in an owned home, plus a few more about who is responsible. This guide is general information and not legal advice. Your lease and Ohio law decide the specifics, and a legal aid office or attorney can help with a dispute.

What Ohio law says landlords must do

Ohio Revised Code section 5321.04 sets out a landlord's obligations. A landlord must keep the premises fit and habitable, keep the plumbing in good working order and supply running water. If a burst pipe or a failed water heater leaves a unit without water or unsafe, the landlord has repair duties.

A lease can add detail, and it can assign certain tasks to a tenant, such as yard work or minor upkeep. It cannot generally take away the basic duties above. If you are unsure, ask a legal aid office or an attorney.

What to do: tenant steps

  1. Stop the water if it is safe, using the valve under the fixture or the main shutoff.
  2. Tell the landlord or property manager right away and put it in writing, with the date and time.
  3. Photograph the damage and your belongings before moving anything.
  4. Move valuables to a dry place and keep receipts for emergency costs.
  5. Contact your renters insurance, if you have it, to report a claim.

Written notice matters. A text or an email creates a record, and a phone call alone does not. Keep a copy of every message and note the response time.

Landlord steps

  1. Stop the source and arrange repairs quickly.
  2. Call a restoration company for extraction and drying, since delays raise costs.
  3. Notify your property insurer and keep records.
  4. Tell the tenant what is planned and how long it may take.
  5. Document readings and repairs for your files.

Rentals around Lorain County

Lorain, Sheffield Lake, Amherst and Vermilion all have rentals, from upstairs flats to lakeside cottages and multi-unit buildings. Water from a leak in one unit often reaches another through ceilings and walls.

In a multi-unit building, report the loss to the property manager at once, since the source may be in a unit nobody is home in. The ceiling repair page explains why water often shows up below the leak.

Renters insurance and the landlord's policy

A landlord's policy generally covers the building, not a tenant's belongings. Renters insurance covers belongings from covered events, and many policies exclude flood. Check your policy, take photos and list items with value.

Getting the unit dry quickly

Speed is the shared interest of tenant and landlord. A unit that dries in days can be repaired and re-let sooner, and a tenant can return sooner. A unit that stays wet for weeks can lose flooring, drywall and sometimes trim.

  • Landlords should call a crew the same day they learn of the loss.
  • Tenants should give access for extraction and drying equipment.
  • Both sides should agree on how to handle belongings that are in the way.
  • Keep a log of dates, visits and what was said.

If equipment is running in your unit, expect noise and some warmth, which is normal. Ask how long the crew expects to be there and when readings will be taken.

Belongings and temporary housing

Ask the landlord in writing whether the unit is safe to stay in and who covers a hotel if it is not. Lease terms and the law decide the answer, and a legal aid office can explain your rights. Keep receipts for any extra costs while the unit is repaired.

Mold, odors and delays

If a repair drags on and the unit stays damp, mold can develop. Put the delay in writing, keep photos and ask what the timeline is. This site makes no health claims, so ask a physician about symptoms, and ask your local health department or legal aid about options if repairs do not happen.

Tenants in Vermilion and Amherst should follow the same steps. A calm, written record is the strongest tool both sides have.
