AC leak water damage in Lorain most often shows up as a stain near a vent, a wet ceiling below an attic unit or a puddle around the air handler. Turn the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate, put a towel or pan under the drip and call. The line is answered 24/7, though most AC leaks are a stain, not an emergency flood.

Why air conditioners leak

Cooling the air pulls water out of it, and that water leaves through a condensate line. The line can clog with algae and dust, the drain pan can rust or crack, and a frozen coil can melt and overflow the pan. A float switch can stop the system when the pan fills, but only if it is installed and working.

What the dry-out involves

The leak is a clean-water loss in the early days, which helps. A crew uses a moisture meter to find the wet area, then dries the ceiling board, insulation and framing with air movers and a dehumidifier. If the leak ran for weeks, drywall may have to be replaced and mold can grow, so early action pays off.

  • Shut the system down so water production stops.
  • Have an HVAC technician clear the line or replace the pan, and install a float switch.
  • Dry the ceiling and attic insulation where water pooled.
  • Replace drywall that is soft, crumbling or stained through.
  • Retest moisture before closing and painting.

Signs the leak has been going on a while

A single overflow leaves a fresh, wet stain with a clear edge. A slow leak leaves layers, with rings that grow outward and darken at the center. Look for peeling paint, a sagging corner near a vent, rust on screws in the ceiling and a smell like damp cardboard.

If the air handler sits in an attic, check the insulation around it. Flattened, dark or clumped insulation tells you water has been there for a while, and the framing under it deserves a moisture reading before anything is closed up.

What to do while you wait

  • Leave the thermostat off and run fans to move air through the room.
  • Empty any pan or bucket under the drip and keep a towel on the floor.
  • Take dated photos of the stain and the unit.
  • Do not paint or caulk over the stain until it has been tested.
  • Book an HVAC technician to clear the line and check the float switch.
