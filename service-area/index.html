Water Damage Service Area Around Lorain, OH

One dispatch line covers Lorain and the nearby lakeshore and inland towns. Choose your town for local notes, ZIP codes and what to ask your city.

Call (440) 276-9091See all services

Lorain sits at the mouth of the Black River on Lake Erie. Sheffield Lake lies to the east along the shore, Amherst sits inland to the south and Vermilion lies to the west on the Vermilion River. Each town has its own building department, its own water source and its own rules for permits and contractors, which is why the town pages exist.

The dispatch line is the same for every town, and it is answered every hour of every day. Same-day response is offered when available.

Lorain ZIP codes include 44052, 44053 and 44055. Sheffield Lake is 44054, Amherst is 44001 and Vermilion is 44089. Homes in nearby Avon Lake and North Ridgeville can call the same line, and so can homes in Elyria.

Towns we serve

Lorain

The home city. Water damage calls here run from lakefront streets to the Black River and the south side. See water extraction and basement cleanup for the most common starting points.

Sheffield Lake

A Lake Erie shoreline city in ZIP 44054, with its own building department and a water supply that comes mainly from Avon Lake.

Amherst

An inland city in ZIP 44001 that buys water from Lorain and Elyria and issues permits through an online portal.

Vermilion

A harbor town in ZIP 44089 on Lake Erie and the Vermilion River, in both Erie and Lorain counties.

Neighborhoods inside Lorain

Inside the city itself, three neighborhoods have their own pages.

South Lorain

Along the southern edge of the steel mill, with sewer backup and heavy rain flooding reported by the city and local news.

Downtown Lorain

The central district near the Black River mouth, where shared buildings and storefronts change who to call first.

Charleston Village

A historic neighborhood near the river mouth and Broadway, with notes on drying plaster, wood and older materials.

How to use the town pages

Each town page lists what to expect from the local building department, how the water supply works and what kinds of losses are common there. Use it to prepare questions for your insurer and your city. If a rule is not listed, the page says to ask the city rather than guess.
