What storms do to a house

Wind lifts shingles and flashing, and rain follows. Water enters through the roof, around chimneys and vents, through broken windows and along damaged siding. Lake effect storms off Lake Erie can drive rain sideways at walls and windows, which wets places that rain never reaches in an ordinary shower.

Lorain has a long weather history, including the 1924 tornado, which formed over Sandusky Bay and struck the city. Storms that serious are rare, but even an ordinary line of thunderstorms can strip shingles and loosen siding.

Stopping more water from coming in

  1. Cover roof openings with tarps secured to dry, sound decking.
  2. Board or cover broken windows.
  3. Move furniture and valuables away from wet areas.
  4. Cut power to rooms with wet ceiling fixtures.
  5. Photograph all damage for your insurer before repair starts.

Drying and repair after storm water

Rainwater is cleaner than flood water, though it picks up roof debris and insulation as it moves. Crews extract, remove wet insulation and ceiling board, and dry framing with air movers and dehumidifiers. After readings reach the dry standard, repairs and roofing can be completed.

Wet ceilings are the most common result, and the ceiling repair page explains the steps. Water that rose from the ground instead of falling from above is covered on the flood damage page and the basement page.

What to document before repairs

  • Roof, siding and windows from the ground, with close-ups of damage.
  • Interior stains, drips and wet floors, with a date on each photo.
  • Damaged belongings, listed with approximate age and value.
  • Receipts for tarps, boards and emergency help.
  • The time the storm hit and any weather alerts you received.

Weather warnings for the area are issued by the National Weather Service in Cleveland, and a screenshot of the alert helps date the event. Keep the paperwork together in one folder, because a claim moves faster when the pieces are easy to find.

What storm cleanup costs depend on

  • Size of the opening. Larger openings let in more water.
  • Time before protection. Fast tarping limits the loss.
  • Attic and ceiling materials. Wet insulation adds removal.
  • Rooms reached. Water often travels beyond the first stain.
  • Roof and exterior repair. A roofer or contractor prices that separately.
