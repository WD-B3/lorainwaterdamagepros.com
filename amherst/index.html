The sandstone city

By the late 1800s Amherst was called the "Sandstone Center of the World" for its quarries, and the settlement at Beaver Creek dates to 1811. Sandstone shaped many older foundations and walls in town, and stone and block foundations let water move differently from poured concrete.

If you live in an older Amherst home, ask a contractor what your foundation is made of before choosing a repair. Water that seeps through stone is handled differently from a leak at a cold joint in concrete.

What to do when water is spreading in Amherst

Do the following while you wait for the dispatcher to answer. They take a few minutes and can limit the loss.

Shut off the water

Close the main valve where the line enters the house, or the valve under the fixture that failed.

Mind the panel

Power to wet areas goes off only if the panel is dry. Water at an outlet or panel means calling the utility or 911 first.

Raise belongings

Move furniture legs onto foil, lift boxes and electronics and take up small rugs.

Avoid drain water

Keep children, pets and yourself out of water that came from a drain or toilet.

Permits and contractors in Amherst

The Amherst Building Department on Park Avenue issues building, electrical, plumbing and HVAC permits through an online portal, and it requires every contractor to register. Plumbing inspections run on set weekday evenings, so a plumber should schedule around that. Ask the department which repair steps need a permit in your case.

Water damage rarely needs a permit for the drying itself, but rebuild work such as new plumbing, electrical or structural changes often does. Pulling a permit before work starts is cheaper than fixing it after an inspection.
