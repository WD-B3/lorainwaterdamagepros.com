How Dangerous Is Black Mold? A Lorain, OH Guide

Black mold gets a lot of attention online. This guide sticks to what a restoration company can honestly say, and points to the right people for health questions.

Call (440) 276-9091See all services

By the Lorain Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

Searches for black mold usually come from a person who has seen dark growth and wants to know what it means. A restoration company is the wrong source for health advice, so this guide does not make health claims. It explains what can be known from the surface, what testing can tell you and who to ask about symptoms.

Color does not identify mold

Mold can be white, green, gray, brown or black, and one species can show several colors. A dark patch on a wall or a ceiling may be mold, but it could also be soot, dirt, staining from water or a plumbing leak. You cannot name a species by color alone.

What a restoration crew can tell is whether a surface is wet, whether a material is damaged and whether growth is likely to continue. Those facts decide what to do next, whatever the label.

What testing can and cannot tell you

OptionWhat it showsLimits
Visual inspectionWhere growth is and how far it extendsCannot name species
Moisture mappingWhere materials are wetDoes not detect growth
Air or surface sampling by an independent inspectorTypes present and amounts compared with outdoor airResults depend on how and when samples were taken

Testing is optional in many cases. If there is visible growth and a known water source, the next step is to fix the moisture and remove affected materials. An independent inspector is the right person to ask about sampling, and a restoration company can explain what the results would change.

Who to ask about symptoms

If you or someone in your home has symptoms you connect to the indoor air, ask a physician. Public health agencies also publish guidance on mold and indoor moisture. This site cannot say what mold does to a person, and any claim of that kind should come from a medical source.

What to do while you wait

  • Keep the area closed off and avoid disturbing the growth.
  • Stop any active leak or condensation source.
  • Run a dehumidifier in the room if the air feels damp.
  • Do not sand, scrub or fan the area.
  • Take dated photos for your records.

Where dark growth tends to show up locally

In homes near Lake Erie, dark growth often appears on north-facing walls, in bathrooms without a working fan, around basement windows and in crawl spaces. Cool surfaces collect condensation, and condensation is moisture.

The crawl space page explains how a damp floor system can feed growth that is out of sight.

What to record before anyone arrives

Write down when you first saw the growth, what the room smells like and whether the area has been wet before. Note any recent leak, roof repair or plumbing work nearby. These details help a contractor find the moisture source faster than the growth alone does.

Measure the patch roughly and photograph it with something for scale, like a coin or a tape measure. A second photo a week later shows whether it is spreading or stable. That comparison is more useful than any guess about color.

Questions worth asking any contractor

  • Will you find and report the moisture source, not just the growth?
  • Will there be containment and a written scope before work begins?
  • How will you confirm the area is dry afterward?
  • Who handles testing if I want an independent opinion?
  • What will you remove, and what will you clean and keep?

A good answer is specific and mentions moisture readings. Be cautious of anyone who promises that growth will never return, because no one can promise that if the area gets wet again. Honest contractors talk about moisture control and verification instead.

When removal is part of the plan

If growth sits on drywall, insulation or carpet, those materials are usually removed. Framing is cleaned and dried. The work is done under containment so spores stay in the work area, and a final check confirms the moisture readings. The mold remediation page describes the process.
