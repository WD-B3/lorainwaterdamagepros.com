What a stain or sag is telling you

A brown ring means water has passed through drywall and left minerals and dirt behind. A bulge or sag means water is trapped on top of the ceiling board, and it can fall as a section without warning. Wet drywall is heavy, so a collapse can injure someone and make the loss much larger.

The source can be a roof leak, an upstairs bathroom, a supply line, an AC drain or condensation in an attic. Finding it comes before repair, because a fresh ceiling over a live leak will stain again.

How the repair runs

  1. Stop the source or arrange a roofer or plumber if it is not stopped.
  2. Relieve trapped water by cutting a small opening at the lowest point, with a bucket beneath.
  3. Remove sagging or soaked ceiling board and wet insulation above it.
  4. Dry the joists and the space above with air movers and dehumidifiers.
  5. Patch, texture and paint once moisture readings confirm the framing is dry.

Ceilings under an attic can carry hidden insulation that stays wet long after the surface looks fine. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show where it is, which is why crews do not trust the stain alone. Lorain homes of every age have attics and upper floors, so the scale of a ceiling loss depends on what sits above, not on the date the house was built.

What ceiling repair costs depend on

  • Size of the wet area. A patch and a whole-room ceiling are different jobs.
  • Ceiling type. Smooth drywall, textured finishes and plaster repair differently.
  • Insulation above. Wet insulation has to be removed and replaced.
  • Access. Low attic spaces take longer to work in.
  • Source repair. The roof or plumbing repair is priced separately.
