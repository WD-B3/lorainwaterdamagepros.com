Water damage here often involves a building shared by more than one tenant, which changes who has to be called and how fast the shutoff matters.

Call (440) 276-9091 if water is spreading. The rest of this page explains how shared-building losses work and what to prepare.

Why a shared building changes the plan

In a building with several tenants, one failed supply line or roof leak can send water through ceilings and walls into neighboring spaces. The first questions are practical ones. Where is the shutoff valve for this unit, who has the key to the mechanical room, and who is the owner or manager to notify?

Who pays for what depends on the lease, the ownership of the building, the cause of the water and the insurance policies involved. A restoration crew can stop the spread and dry the structure, but it does not decide fault. Report the loss in writing to the owner or manager and to your own insurer.

First steps for tenants and owners

These steps are short and can limit the loss.

Find the shutoff

Close the valve for the failed fixture or the unit. If you cannot reach it safely, tell the manager at once.

Keep power safe

Do not touch outlets or panels near water. If water reaches electrical equipment, call the utility or 911.

Notify in writing

Message the owner or manager and your insurer, with the time you noticed the water.

Protect what is dry

Lift inventory, files and electronics off the floor only where it is safe to do so.

Photograph the damage

Take dated photos of wet areas and the source from a safe position.

Documentation and insurance

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe a loss to an insurer, an owner or a manager. Business owners should also keep receipts for emergency costs. Coverage depends on the cause of the water and the policy terms, so ask your insurer before assuming anything is or is not covered.
