Flood damage cleanup in Lorain begins with safety, and the line is answered 24/7 when water has entered a building from outside. Stay out of standing water near outlets and appliances, do not walk through water you cannot see the bottom of, and keep children and pets away. Call before moving anything heavy, and photograph the water line on the walls.

Why flood water is handled differently

Flood water arrives with soil, debris and whatever the ground and the drains carried. That is why restoration treats it as contaminated from the start. Porous materials that sat in it, such as carpet pad, insulation and wet drywall, are usually removed rather than dried, and hard surfaces are cleaned and treated.

Lorain has the lake on its north edge and the Black River meeting the lake at its east side. Wastewater from about three quarters of Lorain and from Sheffield Lake flows to the Black River Wastewater Treatment Plant, which has a design capacity of 15 million gallons a day and can treat 35 million in wet weather. When heavy rain overwhelms drains, water can back up into low basements and first floors.

What the cleanup involves

  • Pumping and extraction of standing water, starting with the deepest areas.
  • Removal of porous materials that absorbed contaminated water.
  • Cleaning and treating hard surfaces and framing that stay in place.
  • Moisture mapping, then drying with air movers and dehumidifiers.
  • Documentation with photos and readings for your insurer.

Flood and insurance in Ohio

The Ohio Department of Insurance says flood coverage is typically not included in homeowners, renters or business policies and is bought separately, usually with a 30-day waiting period before it takes effect. That means a standard policy often does not pay for water that came in from outside. Pull your policy and look for a separate flood endorsement or a National Flood Insurance Program policy.

To learn whether a property sits in a mapped flood zone, check the FEMA flood map or ask the city building office in Lorain. Warnings for the Black River come from the National Weather Service in Cleveland, so watch those during heavy rain.

What flood cleanup costs depend on

  • Depth and duration. Water that stood for days soaks further than water that was pumped out quickly.
  • Contamination. Removal of porous finishes adds work and disposal.
  • Square footage affected. Finished space costs more than bare concrete.
  • Contents. Furniture and boxes that must be moved or discarded.
  • Rebuild. Drywall, flooring and trim are separate from cleanup and drying.
