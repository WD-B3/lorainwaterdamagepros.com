What has been reported about water in South Lorain

On the evening of Tuesday, June 17, 2025, heavy rain flooded some South Lorain neighborhoods, according to News 5 Cleveland. A city sewer department spokesperson said the Pearl Avenue and Tacoma Avenue Pump Station was overwhelmed even though its equipment ran as designed. A resident on East 29th Street told the station her sump pump could not keep up.

In July 2025 a Lorain City Council committee met with residents about the flooding. News 5 reported that the utilities director said the city was designing a project of about 20 million dollars to add capacity at that pump station. News 5 described South Lorain as one of the city's oldest sections and the place where most of the flooding has occurred.

These reports describe parts of South Lorain on particular days. They do not mean every home there floods, and conditions differ from block to block. Whether a home has a basement, a crawl space or a slab changes the plan, so describe yours on the call.

What to do first when water backs up

Do these while you wait for the dispatcher to answer.

Stay out of standing water

The city's own page says not to enter a basement with standing water, because of electrical and contamination risks.

Treat backup water as contaminated

Keep children, pets and yourself away from water that came from a drain or toilet.

Call the city for the main

If the sewer main may be blocked, the city sewer department sends a crew to check it.

Photograph from a dry spot

Take photos of the water line and affected items before anything is moved, only where it is safe.

Skip the household fan and shop vacuum

They are not enough for sewage and can spread contamination. Wait for trained crews.

What restoration looks like after a basement flood

The crew first confirms the area is safe, then removes standing water with pumps and extractors. Water from a sewer backup is treated as the most contaminated category under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, so porous materials such as carpet, pad and wet drywall are often removed rather than dried. A technician decides this on site and not every material can be saved.

Drying follows with air movers and dehumidifiers, and moisture readings are taken on each visit until the materials return to normal. See basement water damage and flood damage cleanup for the full process, and storm water damage when heavy rain is the cause.

Insurance and documentation

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope of work can help describe the loss. Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and the terms of your policy, and sewer backup is often handled by a separate optional endorsement, so ask your insurer what applies. Nothing here predicts what an insurer or the city will pay.
