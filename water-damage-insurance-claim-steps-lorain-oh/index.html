Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps in Lorain, OH

A claim goes better when the first hours go well. These are the steps, in order, with notes on what to write down and keep.

By the Lorain Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

A water damage claim is a record-keeping job as much as a repair job. The order matters because some steps cannot be redone once water has been cleaned away. This guide walks through the steps in the order they usually happen, and it is general information, so your insurer and your policy come first.

Step 1: Make it safe and stop the water

Close the main water valve if a pipe is the source, and stay out of rooms where water touches outlets, the panel or appliances. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 or the utility before anyone else. Safety comes before photos.

Step 2: Photograph and video everything

Take wide shots of each affected room, then close-ups of damage. Photograph the water line on walls, the source of the leak and any belongings that got wet. A slow video walkthrough narrated out loud captures detail that photos miss.

  • Take photos before moving or removing anything.
  • Include the date on the images, or note it in a log.
  • Photograph serial numbers of damaged appliances and electronics.
  • Keep any failed part, such as a split hose.

Step 3: Call your insurer

Report the loss as soon as you can. Ask what your policy requires from you, whether an adjuster will visit and what emergency work they will pay for. Write down the claim number, the name of the person and the date and time of the call.

Step 4: Start mitigation

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. That usually means water extraction and drying, tarping a roof opening or moving belongings. Keep receipts for all of it, because many policies reimburse reasonable emergency costs.

Step 5: Make a contents list

List damaged belongings with the item, the approximate age and the value. Include furniture, electronics, clothing and stored boxes. Keep receipts or photos of the items from before the loss if you have them.

Step 6: Meet the adjuster

Walk the adjuster through each affected area and point out hidden damage, like wet wall bottoms or flooring. Share your photos, moisture readings and the written scope of work. Ask when to expect a decision and what happens if your estimate and theirs differ.

Why documentation matters near the lake

Lorain, Sheffield Lake and Vermilion all sit on or near Lake Erie, where wind-driven rain and high water can complicate a claim. An adjuster may ask whether the water came from above, from a pipe or from outside the house.

Dated photos of how the water entered, and of the level it reached, answer that question better than memory does.

Step 7: Keep a paper trail

Keep every email, estimate, invoice and receipt in one folder. Log each call with the date, the person and the summary. If a decision seems wrong, ask for the reason in writing and for the policy language it relies on.

Step 8: Finish the drying before the rebuild

Rebuild work should begin only after moisture readings show the structure is dry. A structural drying log gives you proof that the materials reached their targets. Closing walls too early is one of the most expensive mistakes after a loss.

Residents of Amherst and Sheffield Lake follow the same sequence, though each town has its own permit rules for rebuild work.

Mistakes that slow a claim down

  • Throwing out damaged carpet, drywall or furniture before photographing it.
  • Signing a repair contract before you know what your insurer will pay for.
  • Letting a long-term leak go unreported until the damage is obvious.
  • Accepting a verbal estimate with no written scope behind it.
  • Losing track of who said what, and when.

Most of these come down to rushing or to not writing things down. A calm first hour that includes photos and a call to your insurer is worth more than a fast but undocumented cleanup. If you feel pressure from anyone to sign something right away, ask for time to read it.

What an adjuster may ask you

Expect questions about when you first noticed the water, where it came from and what you did next. They may ask for the age of the roof, the plumbing or the appliance involved. Answer plainly, and if you do not know, say so and offer to find out.
