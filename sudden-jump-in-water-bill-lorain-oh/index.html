Sudden Jump in Water Bill and No Visible Leak in Lorain, OH

A high bill with no puddle often means a hidden leak or a running fixture. These simple tests narrow it down before you call anyone.

Call (440) 276-9091See all services

By the Lorain Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

A sudden jump in your water bill means more water went through your meter. Sometimes that is use, like a houseguest, a filled pool or a watered lawn. When nothing explains it, a hidden leak or a fixture that runs all day is the likely cause. These checks help you tell the difference in about an hour.

Step 1: check the meter with everything off

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and the ice maker, and make sure no one is using water.
  2. Find the water meter, usually in the basement, a utility room or an outdoor box.
  3. Look at the leak indicator, the small dial or triangle that spins when water moves.
  4. Write down the reading, wait 30 minutes with no water use and check again.
  5. If the reading changed, water is moving somewhere.

A moving meter with all fixtures off is the best sign of a leak between the meter and your fixtures. If the meter does not move, the extra use may have been real, or it may happen only at certain times, like when the irrigation runs or an appliance cycles.

Step 3: look for hidden water

  • Warm spots on a concrete floor, which can point to a hot water line.
  • Damp carpet, buckled flooring or a musty smell near an outer wall.
  • New stains on ceilings or drywall.
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on.
  • Unusually green or wet patches in the yard.

If you find signs of hidden water, close the main valve and call a plumber to locate the line. Locating a leak under concrete or inside a wall uses acoustic or thermal tools. The slab leak page explains what that looks like.

Questions for your utility

Water in Lorain comes from a Lake Erie intake and is delivered by the City of Lorain. If you live in the city and your bill is higher than expected, contact the utility that bills you and ask about the meter reading and the billing period.

Billing is handled differently in the nearby towns. Sheffield Lake gets most of its water from Avon Lake Regional Water, Amherst buys from Lorain and Elyria and Vermilion runs its own plant, so ask the office that bills your address.

Do not confuse a high bill with a flushing habit

Lorain's water quality report advises running a tap for 30 seconds to two minutes after water has sat in the pipes for several hours. That adds a small amount to use and does not explain a big jump. Compare your bill with the same month last year before assuming something is wrong.

If the meter test is clean

A meter that holds still with everything off points to use and not a leak. Look for changes such as guests, a new appliance, a filled tub or pool, more laundry or a lawn that needed extra water. A stuck sprinkler zone or a timer reset after a power outage can add thousands of gallons.

Compare your bill with the same period last year and with the previous bill. Rates can also change, which raises the bill without a change in use. Ask the utility to explain each line.

What to do about a confirmed leak

A leak before the meter, in the line from the street, is generally the utility's side. A leak after the meter is yours. Fix the source, then check for water damage along the path, since a slow leak soaks drywall, flooring and framing. The appliance leak page shows common hidden spots.

Insurers often treat long-term leaks differently from sudden ones, so photograph readings and keep plumber reports. Homes in Amherst and Sheffield Lake follow the same sequence of checks.
