How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Lorain, OH

Photos and papers are often the items people care about most. The first day decides what can be saved, and the steps are simpler than most people expect.

By the Lorain Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

Wet photos and documents deteriorate quickly, and mold can begin to grow within a few days in damp conditions. The aim of the first day is to stop the clock, either by drying items right away or by freezing them until you can deal with them. This guide covers the common cases for family papers, and it applies to basement floods and pipe leaks alike.

First, a safety check

Do not enter a room with standing water near outlets, the panel or appliances. Water from a drain or from outside is contaminated, so wear gloves and boots when handling anything it touched. If the water was sewage, discard paper that touched it or ask a specialist before trying to save it.

Photos

  1. Handle photos by the edges and keep them from sticking to each other.
  2. Rinse mud or dirt gently in clean water, without rubbing the surface.
  3. Lay photos face up on paper towels, clean cloth or a screen to dry in a room with moving air.
  4. If photos stick together, do not pull them apart while dry. Soak briefly in clean water and ease them apart.
  5. If you cannot deal with them within a day or two, place them in a freezer bag, separated by wax paper, and freeze.

Wet prints often dry with minor curling. Negatives and digital files are more resilient than prints, so check any copies stored elsewhere. Do not use a hair dryer on high heat, since it can crack the emulsion.

Papers, books and documents

ItemWhat to do
Loose papersAir dry flat, interleaved with clean blotting paper, or freeze
Bound booksStand upright with pages fanned slightly, or freeze
Important documents such as titles and certificatesFreeze first, then ask a document restoration specialist
Ink that runs when wetDo not rub, blot gently, and freeze if soaked

Freezing is useful because it stops mold growth and gives you time. A chest freezer works, and so does a restaurant or a friend with freezer space. After the emergency, items can be thawed and dried in small batches.

Where papers get wet in Lorain County homes

In lakefront towns like Sheffield Lake and Vermilion, basements and garages hold boxes of old photos and records, and these low spaces take water first. A storage tote on a shelf survives where a cardboard box on the floor does not.

Plastic tubs on shelves, a few inches above the floor, are a small change that protects a lot of paper. The basement page explains what to expect when water reaches low rooms.

When to call a specialist

Rare books, large collections, old photographic prints and documents with legal value are worth professional help. Document restoration specialists use vacuum freeze drying and other methods that are not possible at home. Ask your insurer whether your policy covers the cost.

How to dry papers at home, step by step

Set up a clean, dry surface in a room with a fan and a dehumidifier. Cover tables with clean paper towels or blotting paper, and change them when they get damp. Turn pages gently every few hours so air reaches both sides.

Be patient with thick books and files. They can take days to dry, and rushing with heat can warp covers. When they feel dry to the touch, press them flat under a weight for a day to take out waves.

What to do with the rest of the wet belongings

Photos and papers are only part of the picture. Clothing, fabric and books follow the same rule: dry them quickly or freeze them to buy time. Hard items like glass, metal and sealed plastic can be washed and dried, while upholstered pieces and particleboard furniture often cannot be saved after a flood.

Make a list as you go, with the item, its rough value and whether you saved it. The list helps with insurance, and it keeps you from throwing away something that could have been dried. Take photos before you discard anything.

Prevent the next loss

  • Store papers and photos above floor level in sealed plastic tubs.
  • Scan important documents and keep copies elsewhere.
  • Keep a list of what is stored in basements and crawl spaces.
  • Check the sump pump and gutters before the rainy season.

If a pipe or an appliance caused the loss, the same steps apply, and the appliance leak page explains where hidden water goes. Amherst and Sheffield Lake homeowners should apply the same habits, since any low space can collect water.
