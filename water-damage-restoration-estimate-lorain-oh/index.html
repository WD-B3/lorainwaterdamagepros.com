Water Damage Restoration Estimate in Lorain, OH: What It Should List

An estimate is only useful if you can read it. This guide shows what a clear one contains, what changes it and what to ask before you agree.

Call (440) 276-9091See all services

By the Lorain Water Damage Pros team. Published November 9, 2026.

A water damage estimate should tell you what will be done, where and why. It should separate emergency work from rebuild work, and it should explain how the price was built. Scope and price are explained before work starts, and you should expect the same from any company you call.

What a clear estimate contains

  • The address, date and cause of loss as understood at that point.
  • The areas affected, listed room by room.
  • Emergency services such as extraction, with how long they are expected to take.
  • Drying equipment, the number of units and the expected days.
  • Demolition and disposal, with the materials to be removed.
  • Cleaning of surfaces and contents.
  • Repair and rebuild items, kept separate from the emergency work.
  • Exclusions and what could change the price.

Look for plain language. If a line says only "mitigation" with a single price, ask what is in it. A good estimate reads like a plan, not a lump sum.

How prices are usually built

ItemUsual basisWhat affects it
ExtractionTime or square footageDepth, water type, access
Drying equipmentUnits multiplied by daysMaterials, square footage, readings
DemolitionSquare or linear footageMaterials removed, disposal
CleaningArea or hoursWater category, contents
RebuildMaterial and labor by itemFinishes, matching, trim

The most variable number is the drying time. A crew cannot know it on day one, so the estimate states an expected range and repeats readings each day. If the drying goes longer than expected, ask to see the readings that explain it.

Emergency work and rebuild work are different estimates

Emergency work protects the building. Rebuild work puts it back together. You may choose a different company for each, and you can ask for them to be priced separately. An estimate that bundles them should still list them separately.

What can change an estimate

  • Hidden damage found when a wall or floor is opened.
  • Water that turns out to be contaminated.
  • More days of drying than expected.
  • A change in what the insurer will pay for.
  • Materials that are no longer available and must be matched.

A good company will tell you when something changes and will explain it before doing the work. If a price jumps without an explanation, ask for the readings and photos that support it.

Ohio estimates and local permits

The Lorain building office issues plumbing, electrical and demolition permits and handles contractor registration. If your estimate includes work that needs a permit, ask who will pull it and whether the cost is listed.

In Amherst all contractors must register with the city, and in Sheffield Lake contractors register each year. A registered contractor should be able to say so.

Why two estimates can differ by a lot

Companies measure rooms differently, choose different equipment counts and make different assumptions about hidden damage. One estimate may assume walls can be dried in place while another assumes they must be opened. Neither is wrong until the readings come in.

When two numbers are far apart, call both companies and ask what each assumed. The answer usually shows which estimate is more cautious and which is more optimistic.

Red flags and green flags

Looks like a problemLooks like a good sign
One lump sum with no detailItemized by room and task
Pressure to sign todayTime to read and ask questions
Promise of insurance approvalHonest note that your insurer decides
No mention of moisture readingsReadings included and repeated daily
Rebuild bundled with emergency workPhases listed separately

No single item makes a company bad, but a pattern should slow you down. If three or four entries in the left column describe your estimate, ask for a revised version before you sign.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. What is included in the emergency work, and what is not?
  2. How many drying units and how many days are expected?
  3. What happens if hidden damage turns up?
  4. Are permits needed, and who handles them?
  5. What documents will I get for my insurer?

You are entitled to answers in writing. If a company will not put its scope on paper, consider that a signal. The water extraction page explains what the first visit looks like.
